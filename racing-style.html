Chapter 02 — The Rise

FROM KARTS
TO CROWNS

Born Into Speed

Max Emilian Verstappen was born on 30 September 1997 in Hasselt, Belgium, into a family where motorsport wasn't a hobby — it was oxygen. His father, Jos Verstappen, was a Formula One driver who competed in 106 Grands Prix. His mother, Sophie Kumpen, was a decorated champion kart racer. Racing wasn't something Max chose. It was inevitable.

By the age of two, Max was on a quad bike. At four, he was karting. At seven, he won his first race. By 15, he held three simultaneous international karting titles — two European championships and a World championship — something never done before in the history of karting.

The path from go-kart prodigy to Formula One legend wasn't just fast. It was historically unprecedented at every single step.

2013
Karting
The Karting Prodigy
At 15, Max won the World KZ Championship while simultaneously holding two European karting titles — three international titles at once, a feat never accomplished before. Red Bull noticed. The entire motorsport world noticed.
3 Simultaneous World / European Titles
2014
FIA F3
F3 Rocket
Bypassing junior formulae, Max went straight into the FIA European F3 Championship. In his rookie season he finished third overall, winning six consecutive races. He joined the Red Bull Junior Team mid-year and drove in F1 free practice at Suzuka at 17 years and 3 days old.
Youngest F1 FP1 Participant
2015
Toro Rosso
History at 17
Signed by Scuderia Toro Rosso — the youngest F1 driver ever to start a race at 17 years, 166 days. Despite a steep learning curve, he showed breathtaking raw speed. Won Rookie of the Year, Personality of the Year, and Action of the Year at the FIA Awards. In just his first season.
Youngest F1 Race Starter Ever
2016
Red Bull
The Spanish Earthquake
Promoted to Red Bull mid-season after four races with Toro Rosso. On debut at the Spanish Grand Prix, 18-year-old Max Verstappen won. He became the youngest race winner in F1 history. That single afternoon changed the sport forever. Then came Brazil — his rain masterclass from P16 to P3 in 14 laps remains one of the greatest drives anyone has ever witnessed.
Youngest F1 Race Winner Ever
2017–20
Red Bull
The Building Years
Racking up wins and podiums but denied titles by the Mercedes superiority of Hamilton and Bottas. 10 race wins, 42 podiums across four seasons. Growing in maturity, in race craft, in tyre management — becoming complete. The frustration of having the ability but not quite the machinery only sharpened him.
10 Wins · Sharpening the Blade
2021
Red Bull
The Greatest Fight in F1 History
Equal machinery. Hamilton vs Verstappen. 22 races decided by 8 points. The finale in Abu Dhabi, the Safety Car restart, the final lap overtake — one of sport's most explosive conclusions. Max became the first Dutch Formula One World Champion. The Netherlands went wild. The world was split. Max was born.
First World Championship · First Dutch Champion
2022
Red Bull
Dominance Begins
New regulations. RB18. A machine built for Max. 15 wins — a new record at the time. He wrapped up the title in Japan with 4 races left. The points record shattered. Michael Schumacher's record of wins in a season? Just a warm-up.
15 Wins · Second Championship
2023
Red Bull
The Most Dominant Season in F1 History
19 wins from 22 races. 10 in a row. 575 points. 21 podiums. Words run out. Statistics feel inadequate. The RB19 and Max Verstappen became the most unstoppable combination the sport has ever seen. Even people who weren't fans had to admit they were watching something transcendent.
19 Wins · All-Time Records Obliterated · Third Crown
2024
Red Bull
The Fighter's Title
McLaren had the faster car from Miami onwards. Norris mounted a stunning challenge. But Max won 9 races — including a legendary drive from P17 in São Paulo in torrential rain. He clinched his fourth title in Las Vegas with two races left, driving for a third-placed constructor. Pure will. Pure genius.
4th Consecutive Championship · Greatest Drive From P17
2025
Red Bull
The Comeback That Fell 2 Points Short
Down 104 points to Oscar Piastri after Hungary. Six wins in the final nine races. A title fight going to the final lap of the final race in Abu Dhabi. Max won the race. But Norris, finishing second, took the championship by 2 points. The greatest comeback that wasn't enough — but proved that in any equal machine, Max Verstappen is simply unconquerable.
P2 By 2 Points · The Greatest Near-Miss
